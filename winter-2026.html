Winter bags 2026 · Everett, Washington

Fresh socks are basically gold.

At a Seattle bus stop, a man next to me wrung out his socks, put them in his bag, and pulled on a dry pair. We said nothing. But I learned something that day, and it stayed with me.

That winter it became 24 backpacks for people living outside in Everett. This one is 96 — same method, bought one item at a time, with every price on the page.

Built for dignity first

Every item has to earn its place twice — once for being useful, and once for how it lands when someone opens the bag.

  1. The wipes are unscented

    All of them. A bag shouldn't announce itself across a room, and nobody should be identifiable by what they were given.

  2. A discreet zip bag and a disposal bag

    Managing a body outdoors shouldn't require an audience. These two cost almost nothing and they change what a day feels like.

  3. Menstrual supplies past the token three pads

    The first list carried three. It got upgraded mid-drive to a full-cycle set, because half a solution isn't one.

  4. A pen, a notepad, and a printed resource sheet

    Supplies for a person with plans, appointments and phone numbers to keep — not just a body to keep warm.

  5. Everything is new

    Bought in sealed cases, nothing handed down, nothing anyone else finished with first.

The point was never the socks. It's that somebody opens a bag and can tell it was packed by a person who thought about them — which is not a common experience when you're living outside.

Where the money goes

Short version: to the item you picked, and nothing else.

  • GoodsThe listed price is the real case price from the supplier, plus card processing, plus shipping where shipping isn't free. Nothing is padded on top.
  • OverheadNone. Nobody is paid, there is no office, and no part of a contribution covers admin.
  • TimingAn item is ordered once it's fully funded, and items are bought in the order they're listed, working down from the top. Partial funding sits against its item and waits.
  • OvershootAn item stops accepting money the moment it's paid for, so overshooting is only possible in the seconds when two people are checking out at once. If it happens, the extra moves to the next unfunded item down the list — or you can ask for it back. You choose which at checkout, so nobody has to chase you about it afterward.
  • DeliveryCases ship to a storage unit in Everett, get sorted into 96 identical bags, and go out by hand.

Things worth knowing

Roots Worth Tending is not a registered 501(c)(3). It's a small volunteer effort, not an incorporated charity. Your contribution is not tax-deductible, and you shouldn't claim it as one.
